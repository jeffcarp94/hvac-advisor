# v6.6.2 control and model audit

The reported 58°F bedroom / 57°F front case exposed two problems. The planner could lower a recovery setpoint to improve later bedroom comfort, and the single-mode simulator implemented ideal duty modulation at the setpoint. With the installed 2°F differential, an idle thermostat set to 58°F at a sensed 57°F would not yet call for heat under the documented control law.

The live two-zone simulator and analytical component model now use the same thermostat demand logic. Heating starts 2°F below setpoint and ends 1°F above. Cooling starts 2°F above and is modeled to end 1°F below. Micro-Air documents the gap for all setpoint operations and illustrates heating shutoff. Cooling shutoff symmetry remains an assumption to verify on the installed controller. The manual distinguishes continuous fixed fan from fixed-speed cycled fan. The application currently represents its fixed speed choices as continuous, and its AUTO choice as cycling. Exact fan availability by mode and speed and furnace startup/purge timing on this installation are unverified.

Awake recovery uses the established 70°F heat and 72°F cool settings. Sleep recovery uses the existing 66°F heat and 68°F cool settings. Later phases can independently optimize their settings. A limited action is reviewed at the earliest modeled entry into the bedroom comfort band, capped at the existing 30-minute review time or departure. A complete plan must also pass bedtime readiness to display as qualified.

For each zone, net sensible heat (BTU/h) equals equipment and other gains plus outdoor and interzone transfer. Each transfer is conductance (BTU/h/°F) times the source-to-zone temperature difference (°F). Over timestep `dt` (hours), effective thermal capacity (BTU/°F) times temperature rise (°F), divided by `dt`, equals the net heat at the step endpoint. The model concentrates thermal storage into the two temperature nodes because separate wall, furniture and air temperatures have not been measured. The audit does not use an arbitrary coefficient adjustment to fit the reported output.

Regression inputs are the reported indoor readings and time with explicitly assumed constant outdoor weather. The original issued forecast was unavailable.
